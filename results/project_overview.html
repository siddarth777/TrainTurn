Junction Platform Allocator

A Double DQN agent decides, train by train, which platform an arriving service gets at a synthetic 8-platform Indian railway junction simulated in SUMO, or whether it holds at the outer signal. It is trained to cut priority-weighted station-induced waiting.

Where the savings come from

DQN has the lowest mean wait in four of five train classes. In freight, priority-aware and planned-platform allocation are slightly ahead. The cost is platform changes: DQN moves more trains off their planned platform than FCFS, timetable or greedy, though fewer than priority-aware.

Formulation

How the junction and agent are built

Station layouttwo corridors · shared throats · 8 platforms

Entries that share the approach throat are serialized, and so are exits that share the departure throat. A platform stays reserved from admission until the train leaves the modeled network, plus its clearance time. The outer queue holds up to 2 trains. Overflow waits in an upstream reservoir and is still charged.

State · 229 features

platforms 80
train 20
forecasts 120
9

Per-platform availability, release estimates and compatibility; the current train's priority, waiting, length, dwell and planned platform; 6 noisy arrival forecasts; time of day and congestion. Values are clipped to [−1, 1] and padded to 8 platforms. True future arrivals stay hidden.

Action · Discrete(9)

0 holds the train for at least one minute. 1–8 assigns that platform. Occupied, closed, in-clearance, incompatible or route-conflicting platforms are masked for the agent and baselines alike. Once the 30-minute hold limit is reached, hold is masked whenever a safe platform exists.

Reward · per dispatch minute

r = −(1·W + 0.05·O + 0.2·U + 2·P + 0.2·H) / 10

W is priority-weighted controllable waiting; O and U are outer-signal and upstream train-minutes; P counts platform changes; H counts an unnecessary hold while a platform was free. Incoming lateness is not charged.

Double DQN target

a* = argmax_valid Q_online(s′, a) y = R + γ^d · Q_target(s′, a*)

A transition lasting d minutes discounts by γd with γ = 0.99. True terminals do not bootstrap; time-limit truncations do. The next-state mask is stored in replay.

Limits of this evidence

  • All timetables, delays and disruptions are synthetic and uncalibrated. They are not Indian Railways operational data.
  • One training seed. The bootstrap intervals cover scenario variation only, not how much a retrained agent would differ.
  • The checkpoint was chosen on two validation scenarios, so its validation score is optimistic. The held-out test set is separate and was not used for selection.
  • Only the normal setting was evaluated. Stress runs (severity, closures, peaks, corridor disruptions) have not been recorded, so robustness is untested.
